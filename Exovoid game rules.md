## Character Stats & Rolls

Every character is represented by a set of attributes and skills, each ranging from levels 0 to 8\.  
Attributes can go beyond level 8 only through cybernetic enhancements.

### Attributes

The following describes the attributes that are present in Exovoid.

**Constitution (CON)** \- Describes the overall health and toughness, allowing them to survive even in harsh conditions.

**Strength (STR)** \- The brute physical strength of the character, allowing them to lift heavier loads or strike with more force.

**Agility (AGI)** \- The general speed, reaction and fine motor skills of the character.

**Intelligence (INT)** \- The ability of logical thinking, memory, analysis and solving complex problems.

**Education (EDU)** \- This attribute reflects the overall education, life experiences and available knowledge, and their ability to draw inspiration from those.

**Personality (PER)** \- Describing the charisma, social skills and personal self reflection.

**Coolness (COO)** \- The character’s ability to sort problems through clever improvisation, intuition, or keeping a cool head and remaining unfazed in stressful situations.

### Skills

**Acrobatics (AGI / STR) \-** The character’s ability to balance, jump elegantly, landing or make acrobatic maneuvers.

**Athletics (CON / STR) \-** Physical fitness for sprinting, jumping, throwing and similar disciplines.  
**Command (PER / COO) \-** The ability to inspire, interrogate, intimidate others or to simply come up with a tactical plan.

**Computers (INT) \-** Hacking or repairing computers, as well as using them efficiently is governed by this skill.

**Con Arts (PER / COO) \-** Con describes a character’s ability to navigate complex social situations to convince, lie or negotiate.

**Defense (AGI / CON) \-** This describes the characters ability to defend themselves from harm, by dodging, parrying or blocking.

**Empathy (PER / INT) \-** An empathetic character can put themselves into the shoes of others to understand their emotions and desires, or to assess their motives.

**Firearms (AGI / COO) \-** A firearms expert knows how to modify or repair a gun, and how to use it with great accuracy.

**Gunnery (AGI / INT) \-** A gunner operates the stationary weapons that are mounted on fortresses, vehicles or even starships.

**Heavy Weapons (STR / COO) \-** This skill governs the use of heavy weapons like flame throwers, grenade launchers or miniguns.

**Infiltration (AGI / COO) \-** The ability to remain hidden or to infiltrate others without knowing about your presence. The skill also governs the ability to impersonate others or to masquerade.

**Investigation (INT / COO) \-** Through investigation, a character can put together clues or notice things that are out of place.

**Medicine (EDU / COO) \-** Doctors are crucial to find the right treatment for any kind of wound, illness or poison. They can also help install cybernetic modifications.

**Melee (STR / AGI) \-** With this skill, a character can artfully use their fists or any type of melee weapon, from brass knuckles to a plasma sword.

**Pilot (AGI / COO) \-** The skill covers a wide range of vehicles, from simple motor bikes to large space vessels. The skill only covers their general usage, esp. maneuvering.

**Politics (EDU / PER) \-** With this skill a character may know important figures, but also how to negotiate political intricate situations or how to behave to belong to upper classes.

**Science (INT / EDU) \-** A scientifically inclined character has a great understanding of math, chemistry, physics and similar fields. They may be able to devise a blueprint to modify a given piece of equipment.

**Sensors (INT / EDU) \-** Thanks to this skill, a character can use and read the sensory data from various devices, including drones.

**Skullduggery (COO / INT) \-** The skill covers means to trick someone with a distraction, pick locks,  pickpocketing or other means of theft.

**Streetwise (INT / PER) \-** With this skill, a character may know their way around a city, where to find certain contacts or even black markets.

**Survival (CON) \-** A survivalist is able to make their way through a harsh environment, set up camps, forage for food or just generally survive inhospitable conditions, like resisting illness or venoms.

**Tech (INT / AGI) \-** An engineer can create, modify or repair pieces of equipment, or assess their general state.

**Xenology (INT / EDU) \-** A xeno scientist knows about the demeanor of various alien species, the nuances of their language and politics.

**Zero-G (CON / AGI) \-** Through this skill, a character can navigate zero-g environments with ease, as well as donning a zero-g suit quickly and safely.

### Derived Attributes

Every character is comprised of a number of derived attributes that are used during various gameplay situations:

**Health** \- The general physical health of a character before they begin to suffer injuries from further sources of damage.  
*Health \= 5 \+ CON.*

**Vigilance** \- This describes the character’s ability to notice incoming threats or when something is just generally off. A character rolls attribute dice equal to their vigilance when called for.  
*Vigilance \= 3 \+ (INT \+ COO) / 3\.*

**Heft** \- Heft is added to a character’s damage when attacking with a melee weapon. The bonus is doubled when attacking with a two-handed melee weapon.  
*Heft \= STR / 2\.*

**Edge** \- A player receives several edge points per adventure. A player can use them before any roll to gain a \+3 pool bonus , or after any roll to re-roll all dice \- the new result must be kept.  
*Edge \= 3 \+ (COO / 4\) \+ (EDU / 2).*

**Action Points** \- This determines a character’s overall initiative and speed during a combat encounter, giving them more actions to perform.  
*Action Points \= 3 \+ (AGI) / 2\.*

**Speed** \- This is a character’s speed (in squares) when they take the “Maneuver” action during encounters.  
*Speed \= 3 \+ (CON \+ AGI) / 2\.*

**Cyber Immunity** \- A character with a high cyber immunity can have more cybernetics in them before risking detrimental health effects.  
*Cyber Immunity \= CON \+ STR*

### Skill Checks

When a character makes a corresponding test, they check their skill list.  
Each skill is related to 1 to 2 attributes \- in case multiple attributes are involved, calculate the **average** among them (round up).

Then you roll a number of different dice:

* One standard die. You always have this one, and it’s never modified.  
* One aptitude die per (average) attribute point you have in that skill.  
* If you have skill points, convert one aptitude die into an expertise die instead, for each level of the skill.  
  * Should your skill exceed your attribute level, the higher value determines the aptitude dice, and the lower the expertise dice.

***Example:***  
*Harold has 4 attribute levels in piloting and 3 skill levels.*  
*Therefore he would roll one standard die, one aptitude die and three expertise dice.*

*Kentrall has only 2 attribute levels in firearms, and 5 skill levels.*  
*Therefore she would roll one standard die, three aptitude dice (since her skill is three levels above the attribute) and two expertise dice.*

The GM will mention a difficulty and the skill check needs to show a number of successes that meets or exceeds that number.

Dice have various outcomes that are explained now.

**![][image1] Success** \- These determine whether a check has failed or succeeded.

**![][image2] Explosive Success** \- In case this result is rolled, the player may roll one additional die of the same type. They may then decide whether to keep the new, additional result or discard it.

**![][image3] Trigger** \- Triggers describe positive side outcomes that the character achieved in case of a general success. It may mean the character knows more about the issue at hand, does it faster, with less resources involved etc.  
Be creative\!  
Ultimately, every character may possess talents that require triggers to be spent for specific outcomes.  
It is always possible for a character to **convert 2 Triggers into one additional success**.

**![][image4] Complication** \- A complication may be triggered by the character optionally. This result immediately **adds two successes**, but in that case the victory comes at a cost: The weapon may jam, the character loses an item in a hasty situation or hurts themselves. The DM will describe the complication based on the situation. However it is important that (should the successes suffice) the character achieves the main goal of the skill check.

**![][image5] Botch** \- In case a botch is rolled, the entire roll fails and no other rolled symbols can be used. It also means something mildly bad happens to the character \- treat it similar to as if the character rolled a single complication. If the role does not contain a single Trigger, treat the result as two complications instead.

**![][image6] XP** \- You and your entire party gain an Experience Point, counting towards the next level-up.

### Support Checks

Oftentimes characters may want to collaborate on a given task to increase their odds of success. The GM is the final arbiter to decide if and how many characters can assist in a given task.

If other participants are allowed and are also willing to contribute, they need to spend the same amount of time on the task as the main person doing it.

The supporting characters **roll an amount of aptitude dice equal to half their skill, round up,** (no standard dice, no expertise dice) and may **add all results** to the main performer’s check. Supporting characters always make their check first\!  
Alternatively, instead of contributing with dice, they may **add one of their talents** (as applicable) so that the main person doing the task benefits from it.

If a supporting character doesn’t have any levels in a given skill but their aid would still make sense, they just roll a single aptitude die instead.

## Character Creation

To create your own player character, you will have to go through a number of steps in sequence to determine your various stats. However, before doing so you should picture the type of space adventurer you want to play. Are they a very social character? Do they excel in hand-2-hand combat?  
Furthermore, you should think about their motivations and what opinion they might hold about becoming a secret member of the E1 organization. Did they do it for the kick? Money, influence or connections? Or did they grow up with an innate fear against the alien overlords and are looking for a way to help humanity stand their own ground?

After you have answered a few key questions for yourself, you can begin with the mechanical steps of character creation.

### Career

Choose a single career that your character has spent considerable time in. By choosing one, you gain access to that career’s talent tree, and you may immediately **unlock up to two talents** \- you have to adhere to the usual tier-based restrictions. Unspent talent points can be spent later during play\!

Along with your primary career, you gain a set of **starting skills and gear**. Do not spend your Credits or Assets yet, as these may be altered during your next steps.

### Attributes

You may distribute a total of **28 attribute points** between the seven attributes. It is perfectly fine to leave an attribute with a score of 0\.  
However, during this step **attributes are limited to a score of 6 for three attributes of your choice, and a limit of 4 for all others** \- after this step, the attributes may still increase through other parts of the creation process. During play, you may increase your attribute scores further by unlocking special “Training” talents within the talent trees of your chosen careers.

### Background

During this step, you will determine some of your character’s background. Each outcome should inspire you about the character’s life before they became a member of E1, and will result in some unique benefits.

To perform this step, you roll twice on each table and choose one of the two results \- if both dice show the same result, you may re-roll one of them until you have two different numbers shown.  
You will roll on **Origin, Childhood, Adolescence and then twice on Life Events** (keep in mind that for Life events, you roll two times two results and choose one each), in that order.

**Only once** during this step, a player may decide to **roll two additional dice** for a given table to have more choice between types of events / backgrounds. This can be done **after** rolling the first two dice.

### Further Training

In this step, you gain **30 skill points** that you can spend at your heart’s content. As a starting character, **no skill can be higher than 6**, and improving a skill after level 4 costs 2 instead of just 1 skill point (so the 5th level and beyond have increased costs).

### Starting Equipment

Now you can determine your starting equipment, vehicles or even space ships. Spend your credits within the restrictions mentioned in the previous step. During character creation, you may ignore the rarity of items.  
On top of their starting equipment, every character is granted **1000 Credits** that they can spend freely within any category, be it cyberware, weapons, armor or general items.

If you decide not to spend some amount of credits or assets within a given, restricted category (e.g. cyberware), these credits and assets become regular funds that your character begins the game with.

### Final Touches

Finally, calculate your derived attributes (like cyber immunity, edge points etc.) and give your character a gender, age, name and other info on their background.

### Party Ship

After the entire group has made their characters, as part of any new campaign, they are given **30 Assets**, **plus another 10 Assets per player in the campaign** that they can spend together to buy a space ship including modules. This should enable any group to get a corvette to perform their missions across space.  
Party members are free to **add their own assets** from the creation process to either get a bigger ship, additional ships or to add more modules.

Every ship comes with an amount of **free space suits** equal to the bridge size of the ship’s class.

## Combat Encounters

If a combat encounter begins, all participants immediately roll **1d6** and add the result to their **Action Points** value. This will determine the order of all participants.

The above step is repeated each round and a round ends once all participants have 0 or less remaining Action Points.

The combatant with the highest remaining Action Points value acts first. Should two participants have the same value, the following factors decide the order:

* Higher Coolness attribute first.  
* On a tie, players act first.  
* Should two players have the same remaining Action Points and Coolness, they can decide their order.

Once it is a combatant’s turn, they can perform **one action** by spending the corresponding amount of **Action Points**.

Should a combatant have less Action Points than the action they intend to perform, they can still do it, but they have to subtract the excess Action Points from their result of the following round.

**Free Movement** \- For any action, the character can move one square before or after they perform it \- with the exception of the Maneuver, Disengage, Dodge or Quickstep actions.

### Combat Actions

**Maneuver (4 AP)** \- Move a number of squares equal to your speed.

**Disengage (3 AP)** \- You move up to 2 squares, but do not provoke any Attacks Of Opportunity.

**Quickstep (1 AP)** \- You move up to 1 square.

**Attack (X AP)** \- Perform an attack with your weapon. The Action Point cost is determined by the weapon used.

**Reload (X AP)** \- You reload the magazine of your firearm. The cost depends on the weapon used.

**Prepare (2 AP)** \- You decide to wait and prepare. You gain a stacking pool bonus of \+1 on your next roll, up to a maximum of \+4.

**Use Item (X AP)** \- You use a special item like a medikit or similar. The cost and outcomes are determined by the item used.

**Ready Weapon (3 AP)** \- Take one weapon into your hand that you have ready in a holster or strapped around your back. Throwables (like grenades) don’t need to be readied first to be used \- drawing them is already part of their attack AP costs.

**Dodge (2 AP)** \- This is an out-of-turn action that you can do reactively so long as you still have Action Points. You immediately have to move one square and perform a roll on defense (with a general \-3 pool penalty) to add your successes to the difficulty to hit you. This has to be done before the attacker makes their roll, but after they announce you as their target\!

**Parry (1 \+ X / 2 AP) \-** The costs of this action depend on the costs of the melee weapon to attack with (half the costs, round up). Like dodge, this is an out-of-turn action, but to defend against a melee attack.  
You perform a defense roll (with the \-3 pool penalty) and add the successes to the difficulty to hit you. If you parry successfully and the attacker misses you, you can spend 2 Triggers to perform a **Riposte** counter attack, automatically hitting the attacker for your weapon’s base damage plus Heft.

**Attack Of Opportunity (X AP)** \- The costs of this reaction are equal to the AP costs to attack with your equipped melee weapon. This is a reaction similar to Dodge and Parry, and can only be used if a character within your melee reach attempts to move away from you using a **Maneuver or Quickstep** action, or a free movement. The moment they announce it, you may perform the Attack Of Opportunity and handle it like a regular attack. The target **reduces their speed** for their attempted Maneuver action (or free movement) by 1 for every health point lost (or every injury die they are about to roll).

**Assist (3 AP)** \- You aid the ambitions of an allied character. You either make a command roll or use an appropriate skill / item to aid their next roll. **The difficulty is 2**. In case of a success, they gain \+2 triggers on their next roll, plus one additional trigger for every surplus success.

**Second Wind (4 AP)** \- You rally yourself. Recover one health point for every success rolled on a **Survival d0 check**  \- you can only do this once per day.

**Assess Opportunities (4 AP)** \- You assess your opportunities in the current scene. Make a **Vigilance d2 roll**. If you succeed, you regain one Edge point that has to be spent during this encounter (or it is discarded). Alternatively, you may discover a heroic opportunity based on the number of successes and your creative ideas and the GM’s discretion.

**First Aid (8 AP)** \- You attempt a Medicine check to treat someone’s lost health or injuries (see “Healing” section). Cannot be used on someone who is within melee reach of an enemy.

### Attacks & Damage

When a character attempts to attack another character, they must use an appropriate skill and succeed at a **difficulty 2 check**.  
Keep in mind that the target (assuming they are aware) may attempt to dodge or parry the attack, thus increasing the difficulty of the roll further.

If the attack is successful, the attacker deals damage according to their used **weapon**.

The damage is increased by \+1 for each **surplus success** of the attack roll.

The target must then reduce the damage by the **primary soak value** of their worn armor.  
Afterwards, they deduct 50% of the remaining damage from the **durability** of their worn armor, and 50% from their **health** (the target may round in either direction).

Should the target not have any durability on their armor left, they instead use the **secondary soak value** of their worn armor, and apply 100% of the remaining damage to their health.

In case the health of the target becomes negative, they begin to suffer **injuries**. Health doesn’t actually ever become lower than 0 though.

Every time they suffer a hit, they have to roll for injury. Roll an amount of **injury dice** equal to the damage beyond the heath limit.

Count the number of “**injury**” symbols and then draw a random injury result from the table that corresponds with that amount of symbols.

Additionally, these dice may trigger “**adrenaline**” \- regain one **edge point** for each adrenaline symbol rolled.

Furthermore, injury dice may produce “**cybernetic malfunctions**”. If this is the case and your current cyberware exceeds your cyberimmunity limit, you have to roll on your cybernetic malfunctions table for each symbol rolled on the injury check (see section about “Cyberware System”).

Every injury a player is suffering from **modifies future injury rolls** by adding more dice to each such roll \- these are cumulative\!

### Movement Rules

Whenever a character can move a square during an encounter, they can move diagonally or straight onto adjacent squares.

In Zero-G environments or difficult terrain, the GM may demand a **Zero-G or Athletics check** for a given **Maneuver** action. The character moves one less square for every shortfall on the set difficulty of the roll.

### Attack Modifiers

Certain factors can make it harder or easier for an attacker to hit their target, modifying the difficulty.

| Circumstance | Modifier |
| :---- | :---: |
| The target is within range, but not within optimal range. | \+1 |
| The target is behind cover. | \+1 |
| The target is in a fast moving vehicle. | \+1 |
| Bad weather or lighting conditions. | \+1 |
| Immense Darkness | \+2 |
| The target is noticeably smaller than a human (e.g. small drone). | \+1 |
| You are within reach of someone with a melee weapon. | \+2 |
| Target is prone and at least 10 squares away. | \+1 |
| Target is prone and within 5 squares. | \-1 |
| Target is incapacitated or otherwise not moving. | \-1 |
| Target is unaware or caught off-guard. | \-1 |
| The target is noticeably larger than a human (e.g. large alien or vehicle). *Note: This modifier does not apply during spaceship-to-spaceship combat.* | \-1 |

## Spaceships & Combat

Spaceship combat is a rare and intense event, demanding high alert from all parties involved. When multiple space ships engage in battle, the experience is terrifying and typically leaves no ship entirely unscathed. On the bright side, these conflicts seldom lead to the total destruction of any vessel. Most confrontations conclude when one party is rendered unable to return fire.

Like in regular combat, all combatants roll for **Action Points** and resolve their actions in the same way, but the actions they can take, as well as their outcomes, differ.

### Ship Stations

During a ship combat encounter every participant needs to declare a role or station that they are taking, which describes the actions they have access to.

Assuming or **changing a station requires spending 4 Action Points**.

The stations that are available depend on the type of ship and its available subsystems, but usually include:

| Role | Duties |
| :---: | :---- |
| **Officer** | Gives commands, coordinates efforts and maintains the overview of battle, as well as negotiating with involved parties. |
| **Pilot** | Steers the ship, navigates and performs evasive maneuvers. |
| **Gunner** | Operates a single dedicated weapon that is mounted on the ship. |
| **Engineer** | Runs around the ship to re-route energy or to jury-rig systems for immediate, temporary repairs. |
| **Sensor Operator** | Operates sensors to scan for threats and weaknesses, providing general intel or making calculations for flight & attack paths. |
| **Electronic Warfare Specialist** | Hacks into the systems of other ships and prevents the same from happening to their own vessel. |

### Officer Actions

**Coordinating Command (4 AP)** \- You aid the ambitions of another station on your ship. You either make a **Command d2 check** or use an appropriate skill to aid their next roll. In case of a success, they **gain \+2 triggers on their next roll**, plus one additional trigger for every surplus success.

**Make Battle Plan (4 AP)** \- You assess your opportunities in the current encounter. Make a **Vigilance d2 roll**, using your levels in Command to convert dice into expertise dice. If you succeed, you generate one Edge point that has to be spent during this encounter by any one station of your ship. Alternatively, you may discover a strategic, special opportunity based on the number of successes and your creative ideas and the GM’s discretion.

**Relay Information (6 AP)** \- You can provide allied vessels with crucial intel. If your ship’s Sensor Operator provided any benefits, you can copy those benefits over to other vessels. Make a **Command d2 check**. On a success, one allied vessel gains those benefits. For every 2 additional successes, you can declare an additional ship.

### Pilot Actions

**Maneuver (X AP)** \- The action points spent depend on the size class of your ship.  
You then make a **Pilot d2 test** (which the GM may increase based on other factors like nearby asteroids etc.). If you succeed, you can perform X turns based on the maneuverability of your ship, as well as Y spaces to move forward based on the speed of your ship.  
You can spend surplus successes to provide a gunner of your choice with an equal pool bonus on their next attack during this or the next round.

*Note: A ship can never move forward more than twice its speed per round, no matter how many “Maneuver” actions you manage to perform in a given round.*

**Evade (X AP)** \- The action points spent depend on the size class of your ship, divided by 2 (round up).  
This is an out-of-turn action that you can do reactively so long as you still have Action Points. Make a Pilot check (with a general \-3 pool penalty) to add your successes to the difficulty to hit you. This has to be done before the attacker makes their roll, but after they announced you as their target\!

**Dock (8 AP)** \- You attempt to dock your ship to a ship on an adjacent or same hex. Make a **Pilot d1 check**, but you suffer a pool penalty equal to the amount of hexes your and the target ship has moved during this and the last round.

**Calculate FTL Jump (X AP)** \- The action points spent depend on the size class of your ship, \+2.  
You calculate an FTL jump to a system within range of your FTL drive, and a specified location (e.g. near a known planet). Make a **Science d2 check**. The difficulty may be increased for especially long jumps, jumps into congested systems or when near a gravity well. This action may also be performed by the **Sensor Operator**\!

If the check is failed, the ship immediately draws **4 injury dice** to apply a malfunction. You can spend 2 Triggers to only have the capacitors of your FTL drive drained instead of suffering a malfunction (this is called “emergency venting”).

**Punch FTL Jump (1 AP)** \- After having calculated an FTL jump path, use this action to get your ship to perform the jump \- the ship almost immediately arrives at its destination.

### Gunner Actions

**Attack (X AP)** \- Perform an attack with the mounted weapon that you operate. The Action Point cost is determined by that weapon.

**Reload (X AP)** \- You reload the mounted weapon. The cost depends on the weapon used. The cost is doubled if you don’t have suitable ammo stored in an ammunitions depot module, but the ship’s cargo instead.

**Take Aim (2 AP)** \- You carefully aim or prepare the attack in some other way. You gain a stacking bonus of \+1 Pool on your next roll, up to a maximum of \+4.

### Engineer Actions

**Re-Route Energy (5 AP)** \- You cleverly re-route energy or tap into reserve energy. For this and the next round, the ship is considered to have \+2 energy for each success rolled on a **Tech d0 check**. The energy from all these actions can never exceed 150% of the ship’s total output (in its fully operational form).

**Jury-Rig System (6 AP)** \- You attempt to jury-rig or fix a system so that it temporarily functions. Declare a **malfunction** the ship is suffering from and roll a **Tech** roll with a difficulty equal to the severity of the malfunction. If you succeed, the effects of that **malfunction** (except pool modifiers) can be ignored for the remainder of the encounter (unless the same malfunction occurs again).  
The downside is that your ship gains an acuumulative **\+1 malfunction pool modifier**, which you can prevent by spending 2 Triggers. These additional modifiers are removed as soon as the ship has received proper maintenance.

**Overload (4 AP)** \- You configure a laser-based weapon or railgun to use energy beyond its regular specifications. Make a **Tech d0 check**. For every success, the next attack with that weapon deals \+10% accumulative damage. After that, the own ship suffers 4 damage, ignoring soak and armor durability. This damage can be reduced by 1 for each Trigger spent on the original Tech check.

### Sensor Operator Actions

All these actions require that the ship has a functioning Sensor array installed.

**Calculate Flightpath (6 AP)** \- You perform a **Sensors d1 check**, at \+1 difficulty for each enemy vessel or other complication (e.g. asteroids, space stations etc.).  
If you succeed, your Pilot gains a \+3 pool bonus for this and the next round. You can extend the duration of this benefit by \+1 round for every 2 Triggers.

**Scan For Weakspots (6 AP)** \- You scan for a particular current weakspot in an enemy vessel. Perform a **Sensors d3 check**.  
If you succeed, all Gunners of your own ship increase their damage (after all modifiers) by 30% (round up) for this and the next round against that vessel. You can extend the duration of this benefit by \+1 round for every 2 Triggers.

**Scan Interior (3 AP)** \- You scan the insides of an enemy ship so long as it has its shields down, making a **Sensors d0 check**. You can gain one piece of information for every 2 successes rolled on a Sensor’s check:

* Number of alive & dead biological creatures aboard, and their general size.  
* Looking for a particular type of item (e.g. weapons, illicit drugs etc.).  
* Looking for irregularities in the enemy ship’s system (provides rough estimation about damage).  
* Making a digital 3D map of the internal layout.  
* Assessing the current activity / action of one particular crew member or station (e.g. if a gunner is reading a weapon, or which system an engineer is repairing).

**Jam Signal (4 AP)** \- You perform a **Sensors d2 check**. On a success, increase the difficulty to scan your ship by \+1, and another \+1 for every bonus success. The effect lasts for this and the next round. Only the highest jamming effect counts.

**Scan FTL Signature (6 AP)** \- You can scan for remaining radiation of another ship that has performed an FTL jump recently. This is a **Sensors d2 check**. For every 12 hours that have passed since the jump, you suffer a \-1 pool penalty due to decaying radiation.  On a success, you get to know the location where a ship has jumped to \- you also get intel about the size class of the ship, and its drive signature. If the drive signature is known to you (or a database you own), you can recognize the specific ship.  
The information can be used to calculate an FTL jump on your own to follow said ship.

### Electronic Warfare Specialist Actions

All these actions require that the ship has a functioning Electronic Warfare system installed.

**Hack (X AP)** \- You perform a regular hacking action, but gain access tokens for the enemy ship. The action point cost depends on the firewall level as always, and the enemy ship must be within range of your ship’s scanners.

**Counter Measures (6 AP)** \- You perform a **Computers check** and increase the Firewall of your ship’s system by the amount of your successes, and the IC level by \+1 for every two triggers spent. The bonuses last for this and the next round. Only the highest Counter Measures effect counts.

**Launch Active Defense System (3 AP)** \- You launch an installed active defense system and handle its effects, typically using a **Computers** skill check unless otherwise stated. This is an out-of-turn reaction similar to the pilot’s Evade and can be used at any time so long as you still have Action Points left.

### Spaceship Maneuvering & Arcs

Space combat is represented in hexes on a 2d map. Every ship occupies at least one hex, with larger ships occupying 2, 3 or even more hexes at once.

If two ships share the same space, they are considered to be left, right, in front and behind each other at the same time (so all weapon arcs could fire).

When a ship **moves** (through maneuvering), the pilot has to determine how many turns and how many forward-movements the ship is going to make. **They then have to distribute between turns and forward-movements as equally as possible**.

*Example: A ship wants to make 2 turns and 3 forward-movements. The ship then has to make one forward-movement, one turn, then the second forward-movement, the last turn, and the last forward-movement.*

Each turn a ship performs means a rotation along its middle-axis towards the next hex-edge, left or right.

A pilot can **barrel-roll** a ship at the cost of one turn-movement. Represent this by flipping the ship token. This flips the left & right weapon firing arcs.

Mounted weapons of a ship are usually aligned towards specific **firing arcs** (unless they are turrets with up to 360° freedom).  
Each firing arc represents a 90° angle and it can only shoot at targets within that arc.

### Armor & Shield Quadrants

When a ship gets built, it may have a different distribution of armor plates across its structure, and its own configuration of its shield emitters \- if applicable.

In game terms, when a ship gets constructed, the owner has to **distribute its total shield and armor points among the four arcs** that exist for the spaceship combat. It is viable to leave such an arc, or in this context called **quadrant**, with 0 points of armor and / or shield.

Whenever a ship gets hit by an attack, you need to **check from where the attack struck** the ship \- this is the arc that will take the damage. You then perform the usual calculation of damage by soaking damage against the shields, then the armor & soak, and then the hull. The hull is never based on quadrant distribution, so hull damage is simply global.

*Example: The USC Karakum has a total armor durability of 20 points. Since this frigate is designed to fight with one broadside facing its enemies, the owner of the ship allocates 10 points of armor to the port quadrant, 4 points to its starboard and then 3 points each to the forward and aft quadrants.*

When a ship regains shield or armor points, e.g. through the usual regeneration of shields each round, the commanding officer can simply distribute the regained points among the four quadrants, so long as they maintain the allocated maximum points for that quadrant.

### Multi-Station Occupation

A crew member can decide to take care of more than one station at once. When assuming a station, they can declare their wish to occupy a second station, but have **1 less Action Point available each round** while doing so.

For instance, this allows a fighter pilot to occupy the “pilot” and “gunner” role at the same time and perform actions of both roles.

### Board Computer Aid

Every ship has an AI to aid with various tasks, and they come in different degrees of multi-tasking capabilities and computing power.

A ship’s board computer power is represented in one set of three numbers \- **thresholds** \- and a **multitasking limitation**.

The **multitasking limitation** determines how many people at once the board computer can provide aid to during a given combat round.

**The threshold numbers determine the level of help the board computer provides**. Typically, the less skilled a given operator is, the more the board computer can aid and offset their lack of skills.

If an operator’s skill level is at or below the first **threshold**, they gain **\+3 pool bonus** so long as they actively receive aid from the board computer (based on the multitasking limitation and how it was assigned by the captain).  
If an operator’s skill level is within the second threshold, they still gain **\+2 pool bonus**. And lastly, if someone’s skill is at or below the third threshold, they gain **\+1 pool bonus** \- only the best computers would be able to provide valuable advice & aid to the most skilled pilots, gunners and so on.

*Example: A board computer has the thresholds 2 | 5 | 7\. The gunner has a skill level of 3 in heavy weapons, so their skill level is above the first but within the second threshold. If the board computer gets assigned to them, they gain \+2 pool bonus.*  
*The pilot is an absolute ace and has 8 skill levels. Despite this highly capable board computer, there is nothing of value it could provide the pilot with \- the pilot won’t get any bonus.*

### Auto-Tasks

A board computer can perform some tasks without an operator assigned, so long as their attention is diverted to said task (see multitasking limitation).

**A board computer can fill any station except that of the engineer**\!

In case it does, it has an attribute level equal to its second threshold and a skill level equal to its first threshold.

*Example: If we take the board computer from the above example with 2 | 5 | 7, if that computer was assigned to act as an interim officer, it would have an attribute level of 5 and a skill level of 2 in that task.* 

A board computer has an amount of **Action Points** each round equal to 8 \+ its multi-tasking capacity \- it does not roll 1d6\! If it occupies several stations, it has to divide its action points among the various actions.

### Managing Energy

Ships have limited energy that they require to power various modules, like weapons or their core drives.  
In some cases, the total available energy may be lower than the consumption of all modules combined.

To manage the ship’s energy effectively, any **one crew member** **may re-distribute the energy at the beginning of each combat round**. Systems that gain energy are immediately available for that round, while systems without energy cannot be used.

### Vehicles-To-Regular Damage

When calculating damage of vehicles to a regular scale (e.g. against humans), apply a factor of 3\. The means that a ship weapon deals three times its damage against humans or drones, whereas normal firearms have to divide their damage by 5 (round up) before being applied against a ship’s (or other vehicles) armor and hull. Therefore, most common firearms are rendered useless against larger vehicles.

### NPC Ship Actions

During space combat, the player party will typically fight one or more enemy vessels, or even fight alongside other vessels. It would be excruciating to manage the action points of every single NPC on each ship, and determining which actions they perform.

The entire rule set in this section is mostly there for players to have fun and be tactical, while NPC ship management can be reduced in scope.

For each NPC ship, determine its **pilot** and its **captain**, and only roll for their action points.

**NPC pilots** perform their actions as usual, but cannot use surplus successes from the “Maneuver” action to aid their gunners.

When Captains spend their action points, the GM may spend any amount. Then, a **number of crew members equal to the quality level** of the captain may perform an action that has an equal or lower cost than what the captain spent.

GMs should use these actions typically only to fire weapons, but can put **one action** (for that NPC ship) aside to do any other ship action, including leaving it to perform a reaction during the same round.  
The **dice pool** for all these actions is equal to the **captain’s aptitude and expertise** that is determined once and used for all skill checks.

## Hacking

The normalized protocols that the Galactic Empire brought to mankind, while secure, are still prone to weaknesses and exploits, as is natural law with any technological system.

Hackers know how to exploit the weaknesses of the Galacticnet to manipulate data, control devices or to confuse enemies.

### Hacking Requirements

To hack a device, the hacker needs to have the target device within the signal range of their used master or any of their slaved devices. Instead of being within range of the target device, it is also sufficient to be within range of a device that is slaved to the target master, like a drone, to indirectly gain access to the main device (see section “Slave & Master Devices”).

If a device does not have a signal range specified, assume it to be 5 squares.

In many cases, e.g. when hacking stationary devices, it may be possible to jack into a port to attain a wired connection that is typically planet- or station wide.

There are two ways to hack a device:

**Spontaneous Hacking** \- This happens within seconds as part of individual combat rounds, but makes the Hacker more prone to being discovered or having their device blown.

**Probed Hacking** \- This is a process over several hours or even days. It is a more cautious, safe way to attain prohibited access to the target device.

### Hacking Process

Once the requirements are met and the type of process was decided, the Hacker first needs to spend time.

With **Spontaneous Hacking**, the hacker needs to spend an amount of **Action Points** equal to the Firewall level of the target device. During Spontaneous Hacking, it is not possible to support each other and have multiple hackers collaborate.  
During **Probed Hacking**, the time spent is equal to the Firewall level in **hours**.

After having spent the time, the Hacker will make a **Computers** roll, using the **IC Level** of the target system as the **difficulty** of the roll.  
During Probed Hacking, the hacker gains a **\+2 pool bonus**.

**On a success**, the hacker obtains one **access token**, plus an additional access token per 2 surplus successes.

**Access Tokens are lost over time**\! Access Tokens acquired through Spontaneous Hacking are removed at a rate of 1 per hour. Those acquired through Probed Hacking are lost at a rate of 1 per day.

**On a failure**, the hacker’s device suffers one point of data corruption for each shortfall during the check. If the data corruption level reaches the device’s integrity level, it is fully compromised and unusable until repaired \- additionally, the target device may attempt once to locate the hacker \- it makes a hacking attempt and only needs to generate one access token to locate. Even if no Access Token was generated, the target becomes aware that a hacking attempt has occurred.  
During a **Probed Hacking** attempt, always reduce the amount of data corruption suffered by 1\.

### Access Tokens

A hacker who collected access tokens on a given device can use them at any time (so long as they have a connection to the target device) to manipulate the device or any devices that are slaved to it.

The following is a list of possible actions with their respective cost in Access Tokens (AT), with access tokens that are used being considered spent and removed:

**Scan System (1 AT)** \- Provides an overview of all files and folders within the target system, as well as slaved devices, with a rough description for each.

**Manipulate File (1 AT)** \- The hacker can create, download, delete or manipulate a specific file on the target device (or do a sensible combination of these). 

**System Shutdown (2 AT)** \- Deactivates the entire system, and it takes at least one round before it reboots (so long as a technician is nearby to do so).

**Activate / Deactivate Sub-Device (1 AT)** \- One slaved device (like Cyberware, a security camera, a drone etc.) either becomes active or inactive. The sub-device must remain inactive for this and the next round.

**Hijack (3 AT)** \- The hacker temporarily assumes control over a **slaved** **device** like a camera, door, vehicle, drone or similar. The control remains until this action is removed through the “Cancel” hacking option, or the device is manually rebooted.

**Cancel (1 AT)** \- You remove one access token that the target device has towards one other device of your choice (including your own). Alternatively, you can spend this Access Token to cancel an ongoing action like “Hijack”.  
By using this option, you also get to know how many Access Tokens the device currently controls towards the chosen device.

**Locate (1 AT)** \- You get to know the current physical location of the target device and keep tracking it for one hour.

### Device Repairs

When a hacking device suffered too much data corruption, it is considered compromised: it’s hardware is partially fried and it will be unusable until repaired.

A hacking device that gets fixed before it is fully compromised can be restored using a **Computers d0 check**. This takes 4 hours and repairs one point of data corruption per success.

A hacking device that got fully compromised needs some parts exchanged. This requires a **Tech d2 check**, 1 x “Spare Tech Parts” and a downtime activity. Upon success, all data corruption is restored.

No matter which of the two repair methods above is being used, repairing a device means that **all Access Tokens the device currently holds are lost**\!

### Slaved & Master Devices

Hackers, spaceships or buildings typically have **one master device** to govern all their other devices. For people, it is their cyberdeck, data pad or holo-communicator. For spaceships it’s the board computer, and for buildings it is typically a stationary server or similar.

A Master Device is what is being used to interface with the entire networking system, including the attempt to defend against hackers or perform hacks against enemies, as well as issuing commands to slaved devices.

A master device may have any number of other devices “slaved” under it. This means that these devices are connected to the master and governed by it.

In order to manipulate a slaved device, a hacker needs to access and crack its corresponding master device.

In order **to slave a device**, it either needs a direct wired connection to its master, as is common in space ships or buildings, or it needs to establish an encrypted wireless connection.

To establish such a wireless slave-master connection, both devices need to physically touch each other for a moment (in combat, that is an action requiring 3 AP). After that, this direct communication channel typically has a range of 10km, unless otherwise stated.

### Slaving Cyberware

Apart from a few obvious benefits of very specific cybernetic interplays \- like slaving one’s smarteyes as well as a gun with a smartlink to obtain their benefits \- there are also other advantages of deciding to slave one’s cyberware:

**Slaved Cyberware benefits from better monitoring and redundancy, reducing the risks of harm or damages**.

Whenever a specific piece of cyberware gets affected through cyberware malfunction rolls, and if it was slaved to a master device, the player may **roll any one die**. **On an even result, the damage gets prevented**.

In case multiple pieces of cyberware would get affected at once, the above benefit only applies if **all** cyberware was slaved to a master device.

However, there is a **risk and downside** to slaving cyberware. It gets exposed and vulnerable, as it now can be hacked and manipulated by hostile hackers.

## Healing

Getting harmed is a normal occurrence when one embraces the chaos of skirmishes in space, or delves into missions of espionage on planets.

To recover from suffered injury, there are two methods: medicine or slow, natural regeneration.

**Any attempt to treat yourself incurs a \-3 pool penalty.**

### Regeneration

During each night’s rest (maximum once per day), a character recovers **20% of their maximum health** without having to roll.

For **one injury** of their choice, they can make a Survival roll which is easier or harder based on the quality of the surroundings. The difficulty is equal to the severity of the injury. On a success, the injury is considered healed.

Keep in mind that not all types of injuries can be regenerated through rest, like a lost limb\! This is at the GM’s discretion.

### First Aid

Once per encounter (or right after one), a medic can attempt a **Medicine check** on a given patient. This requires 8 AP and access to medical tools and the patient.

The total successes are counted together, and the medic can distribute them as they like among the injuries the patient is suffering from. An injury that is fully covered in successes (based on its severity) counts as treated \- it is not gone, but its immediate effects can be ignored. The wound still adds its injury modifier though\!

The medic can also spend successes to regenerate 2 health each.

### Surgery

Once per day, a medic can spend four hours with a patient and attempt a **Medicine check** with a difficulty equal to the injury’s severity rating to fully heal and remove it.

Should the character have lost a limb or other body part, then the medic requires suitable prosthetics or lab-grown parts to replace them\!

A lab-grown replacement typically costs 500 to 1000 Credits \- depending on size \- and requires a week to produce and grow.

## Cyberware System

### Cyber Immunity and Installation

Cyberware represents advanced technological enhancements that can significantly improve a character's abilities. However, the human body has a natural limit to how much cyberware it can integrate without adverse effects. This limit is known as Cyber Immunity.

**Cyber Immunity Level:** Each character has a natural Cyber Immunity level, calculated as Constitution \+ Strength. This level represents the total Cyber Immunity points a character can sustain without suffering negative consequences.

**Occupying Cyber Immunity:** Each piece of cyberware occupies a specific number of Cyber Immunity points. As long as the total number of occupied Cyber Immunity points is equal to or less than the character's natural Cyber Immunity level, the character suffers no ill effects.

### Exceeding Cyber Immunity

When the total Cyber Immunity points occupied by cyberware exceed a character's natural Cyber Immunity level, the character enters a state of cybernetic overload. In this state, any malfunction can trigger dangerous side effects.

**Excess Cyber Immunity Points**  
Any points that exceed the natural Cyber Immunity level must be allocated in the Cyber Malfunction Table. For example, if a character with a Cyber Immunity level of 15 installs cyberware that occupies 18 Cyber Immunity points, they would allocate 3 excess points into the table.

**Allocating Points in the Cyber Malfunction Table**  
The Cyber Malfunction Table ranges from 2 to 40\. Players allocate their excess Cyber Immunity points strategically across this range. The closer the points are placed to the middle of the table (around 20), the milder the effects of a malfunction, but the more likely they are to be triggered. Conversely, points allocated near the extremes (close to 2 or 40\) result in more severe consequences but are less likely to be triggered.

### Cyberware Malfunctions

When a character with cyberware suffers a malfunction (e.g., due to an injury, failed maintenance, or a critical failure in an action), they must roll 2d20 and add the results together to determine the outcome.

**Rolling for Malfunction**  
The result of the 2d20 roll is compared to the Cyber Malfunction Table. If the roll matches any of the numbers where the player has allocated excess Cyber Immunity points, a malfunction occurs.

**Malfunction Severity**  
The severity of the malfunction depends on the placement of the points:  
**Central Range (15-25):**  
Malfunctions in this range are relatively minor and might include temporary debuffs, minor system errors, or temporary loss of cyberware function.  
**Outer Ranges (10-14 and 26-30):**  
These malfunctions are moderate and may cause significant debuffs, loss of function in multiple systems, or damage to the cyberware.  
**Extreme Ranges (2-9 and 31-40):**  
Malfunctions in these ranges are severe and can result in catastrophic failures, permanent damage to the cyberware, or even critical harm to the character.

**Example of Malfunction Table Allocation**  
Consider a character with a Cyber Immunity level of 10 who has installed cyberware occupying 13 points. The player must allocate 3 excess points into the Cyber Malfunction Table. The player decides to spread them across the ranges as follows:  
1 point at position 18 (central range)  
1 point at position 5 (extreme range)  
1 point at position 30 (outer range)  
When a malfunction occurs and the player rolls 2d20, any result of 5, 18, or 30 will trigger a malfunction, with the severity and effects depending on the corresponding range.

### Repairing and Maintaining Cyberware

Regular maintenance is crucial to keep cyberware functioning properly and to minimize the risk of malfunctions. Specialized tools and skills are required to repair damaged or malfunctioning cyberware. Characters skilled in **Tech** can attempt to repair cyberware, potentially restoring functionality.

For many malfunctions, to repair them, it is first necessary to take 30 Minutes and succeed at a **Medicine d2 check** to gain access and stabilize the body. The malfunction table determines whether this is necessary or not.  
Failing this Medicine check causes 1d6 damage (ignoring armor), but then it can be re-attempted.

## Downtime Activities

Occasionally, the GM will allow players to perform what are called “downtime activities”. There isn’t really a specified time frame, but typically a downtime activity is something that takes anywhere between 6 and 72 hours.  
Whenever the party has considerable time between adventures, or even during an adventure, the GM will determine how many downtime activities each party member is allowed to perform. Keep in mind that while a character may focus most of their time on the chosen downtime activity, they are still living a life. They might relax occasionally, chat with friends or enjoy a good meal.

While a good amount of downtime activities \- that are available to any character \- are listed below, some additional ones may be unlocked through the acquisition of certain talents:

### Relax and Rest

Maybe your character has seen too much, or came out battered from a fight. If the character focuses on resting, they gain \+3 pool on their nightly rest, and restore an additional 20% of their maximum health.

### Install Cyberware

When a character obtains a new piece of cyberware this typically involves a complex medical procedure that requires some monitoring, calibration and recovery afterwards. Typically, when a character purchases a piece of cyberware, the costs of the medical procedure are included.

When a character wants to install an improved version of a piece of cyberware they already own (e.g. going from the “Alpha” to the “Beta”) variant, the character only needs to **pay the difference in price**, do the procedure, and then will have their cyberware successfully upgraded.

**With this downtime activity, the character can install, remove or replace one piece of cyberware in their body**.

### Train Skill

Your character takes time to deeply learn something about a topic they have an interest in, or something they deem necessary to learn for their very survival or success of the mission.  
When spending time to train, they **gain \+1 skill level, but only in a skill they have 3 or less levels in**. The player should describe how their character practices the given skill.  
However, a character can never gain more skill levels from Training than they have character levels. Therefore, it is adamant that you note down a record of the skill levels you obtained through this method.

### Mentor

Your character focuses on teaching other crew or team members things they have learned, or reflects on past events to extract valuable lessons. Or they just generally try to improve team work by establishing better processes and communication protocols.

Whatever your angle is, roll a d6. On a 5 or 6, you generate one experience point for every player (including yourself).

Hired NPC crewmembers cannot perform this downtime activity.

### Modify Gear

Tinkering with a firearm or an armor is a considerable time effort, as it often requires careful integration, testing, iteration and calibration.

To install a single mod, a character needs to spend a downtime activity and succeed at a **Tech d2 check**. If failed, only the time is wasted.  
To be able to attempt this, access to a **workshop and / or considerable tools** is needed.

**Rail Attachments and Scopes** for firearms never require as much effort, and can be done within mere minutes and without a check.

### Repair Gear

When a piece of equipment, vehicle or firearm was damaged, a skilled Engineer can restore it.

This requires Spare Tech Parts, access to a workshop and a **Tech d3 check**. If failed, only the parts and the time are wasted, but it can be re-attempted by spending another downtime.

If the damage is especially severe, the GM may declare that multiple Spare Tech Parts may be needed.

For drones, each successful repair restores injuries worth a total severity of 6 (you may distribute as you like if the drone has multiple “injuries”), or 10 hull / armor points.

### Repair Ship

A spaceship engineer \- or any other desperate crew member \- may want to restore a ship’s hull, armor or even critically damaged systems, also known as malfunctions.

Each attempt in this downtime activity requires the use of **Spare Ship Parts**, a few common tools, and a **Tech check**.  
The difficulty is 0 when hull and armor durability are repaired, but for malfunctions it is equal to the severity of that malfunction.

When repairing hull and armor durability points, **each success restores 10 points** which the engineer can distribute as they wish.

When these checks fail, the engineer unfortunately wasted their precious time and the expensive Spare Ship Parts by doing the repairs so inefficiently.

For other vehicles, the same rules as above apply.

### Seek Inspiration

**The character gains 2 Edge Points**, even beyond their usual limit. However, a character cannot exceed their edge limit by more than 50% in this way. Surplus edge points can even be taken over into the next adventure in case the downtime happens in between two adventures.

### Networking

The character spends time to get to know people, or to foster connections they already have. They might hang out with them, attend a party or even do them smaller favors.  
When pursuing this activity, the character makes a check with **difficulty 2**, whereas the involved skill depends on the intended outcome.

**Trade & Barter (Con Arts)** \- The character focuses on finding or getting to know a particular vendor, fence or individual who either buys or sells certain items. The next time the character wants to acquire or sell an item, they can **treat the rarity of the item to be 2 lower, plus one for every 2 Triggers**. The bonus cannot be stacked, but be applied to different items. Once the bonus was used, it is lost.

**Butter Up Officials (Politics)** \- The character focuses on getting to know people of power, or to leave an impression some way. The next time the character needs to achieve something in that region where an official’s word would be helpful (e.g. gaining access to a restricted area, avoiding trouble with police), the character **gains \+1 pool bonus in those situations, and another \+1 for every 2 Triggers**. After using the bonus, it is lost. The bonus cannot be stacked up \- only the highest achieved pool bonus counts.

**Draft Crew Members (Streetwise)** \- The character attempts to discover hidden talent in the region. Be it gunners, service people, engineers or other trades. On a success, the GM needs to **present two potential crew members that are up for hire**, including their expected hiring cost (which can be negotiated in a separate roll). For every 2 Triggers, another Crew member needs to be presented. 

**Gather Intel (Investigation or Streetwise)** \- The character focuses on gathering information about a specific target, location, or event. This could involve talking to informants, scouring public records, or simply listening to local gossip. On a success, the character **gains a useful piece of information or a lead** related to their target. For every 2 Triggers, the information is more detailed or reveals additional, unexpected insights.

**Forge Alliance (Politics or Empathy)** \- The character works to establish or strengthen alliances with a specific faction, group, or individual. This could involve attending meetings, negotiating terms, or participating in joint ventures. On a success, the character **secures a favor from the ally**, which can be called in at a later time. For every 2 Triggers, the alliance is stronger, possibly providing additional benefits like mutual defense or shared resources.

**Spread Reputation (Con Arts or Empathy)** \- The character spends time enhancing their reputation, either through self-promotion, impressing influential people, or even orchestrating events that cast them in a positive light. On a success, the character gains a reputation bonus that **grants a \+1 pool on social interactions in the region**. For every 2 Triggers, the reputation spreads further, potentially reaching new areas or increasing the bonus to \+2. Only the highest achieved bonus counts.

**Cultivate Contacts (Empathy or Streetwise)** \- The character actively works on expanding their network by meeting new people and cultivating useful contacts. This could involve attending social events, participating in local activities, or simply making the rounds. On a success, the character **gains a new contact who can provide assistance or information in the future**. For every 2 Triggers, the contact is particularly well-connected or influential.

**Sabotage Rivals (Con Arts)** \- The character uses their connections to undermine or sabotage a rival’s reputation or operations. This could involve spreading rumors, exposing secrets, or interfering with the rival’s plans. On a success, **the rival suffers a setback, giving the character an advantage**. For every 2 Triggers, the sabotage is more effective, potentially causing long-term damage to the rival's standing or available resources.

### Asset & Credit Conversion

As a downtime activity, a character can put in some work to **convert Assets into Credits and vice versa**. This may involve investing into stocks, trading digital tokens, dabbling with the crypto market or even dealing with the often illegal ways of the underworld. Whatever path is chosen, some considerable value is lost in this conversion process.

To obtain one asset, a character can pay 2000 Credits. To gain credits, a character can make one asset fluid (losing the asset in the process), gaining 750 Credits as cash or bank account fiat.

Only one such conversion is possible per downtime activity spent.

### Forge ID

Having access to fake IDs can be crucial in missions around subterfuge, or avoiding consequences on one’s own official ID.  
While fake IDs can be purchased from suitable fences, a character can attempt to create and forge their own.  
This downtime activity requires a number of successes on both **Computers** and **Politics** that is equal to the quality level of the aspired ID. A character can only do one of these checks per downtime, but accrues their successes until they have the required amount.

The quality of the ID determines the difficulty of potential Investigation checks that would discover the forged nature of said ID. The difficulty can never be higher than 5\.

It’s worth noting that a Fake ID is made for a specific person to use, and cannot be transferred.

### Crew Training

The skills of hired NPC crew members can improve over time. To do so, the crew member **and** a player character both invest a down time.  
The player character determines one skill the crew member should become better at, and makes a skill check on that skill. If the successes are equal to the current skill level of the crew member, the skill increases by 1\. Every skill level must be acquired individually.

Alternatively the experience level of a crew member can be increased (e.g. from Trainee to Experienced, or from Experienced to Veteran), if both, a player character and the crew member in question \- spend a total of four downtimes \- no roll is required in this case.

## Character Advancement and Levels

During the course of play, your character will become stronger and acquire new talents and skills.

To reflect your progression, your character has levels, and every character begins the game at level 1\. After each adventure, the GM awards the party with experience points, which are in addition to all experience points gained from rolled checks.  
Whenever a character reaches a threshold of experience points, they achieve a level up \- immediately increase the current level by \+1.

### Level Up Thresholds

The below table provides an overview about the amount of total experience points required per level \- it is worth noting that experience points do not reset upon achieving a new level:

| Level | Experience Needed |
| :---: | :---: |
| **2** | 10 |
| **3** | 25 |
| **4** | 40 |
| **5** | 65 |
| **6** | 90 |
| **7** | 120 |
| **8** | 155 |
| **9** | 200 |
| **10** | 255 |
| **11** | 315 |
| **12** | 385 |
| **13** | 470 |
| **14** | 570 |
| **15** | 700 |

### Level Up Benefits

Whenever your character gains a level, you **gain 2 skill points** that you can freely invest among all skills.  
Keep in mind that getting a level above 4 (so the levels 5, 6, 7 and 8\) cost 2 skill points each to increase.

Additionally you gain **one talent point** that you can use to unlock any one talent of your career(s).  
You can only unlock a talent if you have access to that **tier** of a given talent tree.

The tier is determined by the **total sum of talents that you have already unlocked** within that career.

When your character reaches the **5th and the 10th level** respectively, you may choose **additional careers** that you gain access to \- in these cases, you do not gain any of the starting skills or equipment. You simply gain access to their talent trees.

### Multi-Career Progression

As soon as you have access to more than one career, any time you gain a talent point, you can decide in which talent tree you want to invest it in.  
Some careers have overlapping talents, that means that the same talent may be present in both careers.  
If this is the case and you unlock such a talent in one talent tree, it is automatically unlocked in the other talent tree as well, without having to spend an extra talent point. This also means that this talent increases the **tier** of both (or even all three) careers simultaneously.

## Survival Clocks

Space, hostile planets or other circumstances can provoke detrimental effects to one’s health. This section of the rules describes a universal system to deal with various hazards, and how to reflect the decline of a character’s well-being without requiring extensive dice rolls.

All hazards and circumstances described below make use of one universal “Survival Clock”, but a character tracks their Survival Clocks individually per hazard or circumstance.

The Survival Clock is a meter that has a set limit of ticks, and is calculated as:  
***10 \+ Constitution \+ Levels in Survival***

Based on the situation, the GM will regularly give a player (who is affected by the given hazard) a set number of **ticks**, filling up the Survival Clock for that hazard.

The ticks received can be **mitigated** by a fixed amount (think of it like “soak” for armor), if the player has suitable means, like specialized armor or other creative ways to reduce harm.

If a Survival Clock gets filled up, that circumstance escalates, and the character suffers harm based on the type of hazard or circumstance. Once escalated, reset the Survival Clock until it gets filled up again, escalating further.

A character needs to be removed long enough from a given hazard or circumstance to have all ticks as well as their escalation levels reset \- and with that, the harmful effects they are suffering from.

### Hunger & Thirst

In most cases, players shouldn’t ever have to track this hazard. The GM should only bring it up in harsh survival situations, where it is expected that quenching thirst and satisfying hunger will be difficult or near impossible.  
**Hunger / Thirst Ticks:**

| Situation | Ticks Gained |
| :---: | :---: |
| Character Remains Sedentary | 2 / 4 hours |
| Character Travels | 4 / 4 hours |
| Character Performs Hard Work | 6 / 4 hours |

**Hunger / Thirst Escalation Levels:**

| Level | Effects |
| :---: | :---: |
| **1:Hungry / Parched** | You becomes irritable and distracted, resulting in a \-1 pool penalty on mental tasks. |
| **2:Malnourished** | No natural health regeneration possible any longer, and you suffer a \-1 pool penalty to all checks. |
| **3:Starving** | No natural health regeneration possible any longer, and you suffer a \-2 pool penalty to all checks. You also lose 2d6 health. |
| **4:Starved** | You fall unconscious and will die within 1d6 hours. |

**Means To Mitigate:**

| Strategy / Tool | Mitigation |
| :---: | :---: |
| Ration Food Reserves | 1 |

## Misc Rules

### Falling

When a character falls from greater heights, they are at the risk of suffering injuries.

The GM determines the distance class of the fall, ignoring any fall that is one meter or less:

| Distance in meters | Distance Class |
| :---: | :---: |
| 1-2 | 1 |
| 3-5 | 2 |
| 6-8 | 3 |
| 9-12 | 4 |
| 13-15 | 5 |
| 16-18 | 6 |
| 19-22 | 8 |
| 23-25 | 10 |
| 26+ | 12 |

The character may attempt an **Acrobatics d0 check**, reducing the distance class by 1 for each success.

Afterwards, the character **suffers 5 damage per remaining distance class**, and only considering half the armor’s soak. The armor’s durability is bypassed completely, and all the remaining damage goes against their health.

The GM may alter the distance class based on the **gravity** in the situation: Increase it for planets or spaces with a gravity greater than that of earth, and reduce it for those with less gravity.

### Modifying Equipment

Some equipment, especially firearms and armor, can be enhanced by installing mods.

For **armor**, you can install an amount of mods equal to the armor’s mod limit. You can only install mods that are compatible with that particular armor, and you have to pay each mod separately.

Keep in mind that installing a mod requires a “Modify Gear” downtime activity, whereas during character creation, all mods are considered installed automatically.

For **firearms**, like with armor, you can only install an amount of mods equal to the mod limit. However, there is a key difference. Firearms have mods of different categories, e.g. “Scopes”, “Mechanism”, “Grip” etc.  
**For each category you can choose a maximum of one mod**, and that mod then counts against the overall amount limit of mods for that firearm.

Mods can be removed by investing a “Modify Gear” downtime activity, to then be installed in another armor or firearm respectively. This also frees up a slot for a new mod in the piece of equipment in which the previous mod was removed.

### Firearm Manufacturers

When purchasing any firerarm weapon, you may choose the manufacturer the weapon is from \- unless you want it to be a no-name weapon in which case the base weapon is cheaper \- mods are unaffected.

Every manufacturer adds a unique benefit, and the cost / rarity may be modified as well. The model names in the weapon list are just examples \- so even if a weapon here is listed as “.50 Callisto Eagle”, it doesn’t mean that this gun already has the Callisto advantages factored in. Feel free to creatively change the model name based on your chosen manufacturer.

### Engram Revival

When humans die, relatives or friends (or doctors) might decide to quickly download an copy of the deceased’s personality and memories. These data packs are called engrams and can sometimes be used to “revive” the dead person, either in a simulated VR environment, in a suitable drone or in a specifically made body. Such lab-grown bodies use the dead person’s DNA to reconstruct their former self, while the brain will remain a purely electronic computational unit that runs their engram. **These bodies typically cost 15.000 Credits or 12 Assets**, and take about two weeks to produce.  
Many relatives decide not to revive people in this way, knowing that something is always irrevocably lost, or quite possibly not believing that the new being is truly a reincarnation of the dead, but a mere clone mimicking them.

However, if a player character ever gets revived in this fashion, they need to perform a **roll on the below table** using a d8 to determine in which way their engram is faulty. Perform one additional roll for every 20 minutes that they were dead before the engram was captured:

| d8 Roll | Effect |
| :---: | ----- |
| **1-2** | Lucky\! You are one of the rare cases in which the engram was created without obvious errors. |
| **3** | You lose skill levels worth 3 skill points as your memories have significant gaps. |
| **4** | You regularly suffer from depression or panic attacks. Whenever you attempt to rest or relax, roll a d6. On a 5+, you don’t get any benefits from that rest. |
| **5** | Lose one attribute level of your choice, as your new body is faulty, or you simply didn’t fully adapt to your new self. |
| **6** | Choose 1d3 important NPCs or PCs: you forget everything about them, as partial amnesia wiped out memories related to them.You also reduce your maximum edge points by 1\. |
| **7** | Choose a skill in which you have 5 or more skill levels. You lose all skill levels in it, but regain one skill level whenever you gain a character level, as you re-remember your knowledge over time. |
| **8** | Full amnesia\! Reduce all your skill levels by 1, and forget any knowledge you have about your past or people you have known.You can now spend downtimes towards regaining a lost skill level of your choice for each downtime spent on this. |

### Crew Members

For any ship, it is possible to hire additional crew members to fill in gaps or simply obtain certain benefits.

When the party searches for new crew members (typically by using the “Draft Crew Members” downtime activity), the GM should present the party with a number or different profiles based on their success to connect with available mercs, engineers and other crewmen.

The GM will generate each crew member either individually, or randomly based on the below guides and outlines.

Every crew member has a random experience level from trainee, experienced to veteran. This may be determined using a d6 (with each experience level having an equal chance).  
The experience level also determines the signing bonus that crew member expects \- such a payment is due only once when the crew member gets hired. After that, it is assumed that they keep earning money as a share of the crew’s general activities.

A crew member has all attributes on a specific level based on their experience level, as well as a number of skill points which the GM will distribute as they see fit (based on the profession of the drafted crew member). It is recommended that each crew member only has skill points distributed among a hand full of different skills to keep their profile sleek and easy to use during play.

**Trainee**  
Hiring a Trainee costs 1000 Credits, they have all attributes on level 4, 8 skill points and cannot have any skill higher than level 3\.

**Experienced**  
Hiring an experienced crewman costs 2000 Credits or 2 Assets, they have all attributes on level 5, 10 skill points and cannot have any skill higher than level 4\.

**Veteran**  
Hiring a veteran crewman costs 5000 Credits or 5 Assets, they have all attributes on level 6, 12 skill points and cannot have any skill higher than level 5\. Additionally, a veteran possesses one suitable talent (no tier-related requirements apply).

The GM can roll for the profession of the crew member if they wish (see content sheet under “Crew Members”), and spend the skill points accordingly. Each profession has some suggested items the crew member might bring as their personal belongings, and which they may use to fulfill their job \- for the most part they will expect their new employer to provide them with tools or weapons for the job though.

Each of the crew member professions has a special rule that applies to the ship they are assigned to, regardless whether they are actively using a given station (e.g. piloting cockpit) or not. This reflects certain maintenance techniques, processes or similar things that they employ to optimize workflows on the given ship. The amount of bonuses that can be applied in this fashion is equal to the bridge size of the ship. If there are additional crew members present, the party has to choose for each adventure which of the crew bonuses they want active. The same bonus cannot be stacked\! Each special rule may have a numeric value written as a triplet of numbers (like 1/2/3), whereas the numbers correspond to the experience level of that crew member.

A player character can help a crew member to **develop** their skills. By spending a total of **four down times** (both on the crew member in question and one player character training them), the crew member upgrades to a higher experience level, e.g. from “Trainee” to “Experienced” and from “Experienced” to “Veteran”.

### Rewriting Ship Registrations

You won a space battle and captured an enemy’s vessel? Great\!  
However, space ships and their internal parts follow a rigorous process of registrations, licenses and decentralized ownership protocols.  
Taking a ship doesn’t mean you can use it without issues. Typically, flying a stolen space ship will quickly spark inquisitive questions by officials if no official owner is aboard.  
To make an old ship’s identity disappear and creating a new one is an arduous process that involves various specialists, like forgers, hackers and engineers. It also involves modifying and replacing internals of the ship, for instance to change its drive signature and emissive profiles.

**The process typically costs assets equal to 1/4th of the ship’s value** (including its modules and weapons)**. The rarity of this service is equal to the ship’s rarity \+ 1**.

### Recycling Cyberware

It may often be the case that player characters get their hands on cyberware that is already installed in a deceased body, which they want to extract to use it for themselves or to sell.

Typically, the GM should ask for a **Medicine d2 check** to extract a piece of cyberware without damaging it, with a few minutes to a few hours required for the procedure based on the complexity of the piece.

Extracted cyberware can be sold for 25% of its base value, as opposed to the default 50% sell value of items.

When a piece of cyberware should be installed in a different body, it needs to be modified and adjusted to suit the new host. When a hospital or cyberware engineering lab is tasked with this process, 50% of the original cost of the cyberware needs to be paid\!

However, a player character can do these adjustments on their own by spending a downtime in a med bay (or similar), requiring materials equal to 25% of the cyberware’s base value. They then need to succeed at a **Science check** with a difficulty based on the quality level of the cyberware:

| Quality Level | Check Difficulty |
| :---: | :---: |
| **Alpha** | 2 |
| **Beta** | 3 |
| **Omega** | 4 |

Failing the check means that the character simply requires more time to adjust the piece in question, and can re-attempt it during a future downtime without additional costs.

As soon as piece of cyberware has been modified to suit its new host, it can be installed as per the usual “Install Cyberware” downtime activity.
